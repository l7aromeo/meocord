Themes: design tokens by role, which the bot's answers take their colours, emojis and button styles from.

- `meocord/interface` has the theme's types: `ThemeColors` (`primary`, `neutral`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info`), `ThemeEmojis` (`loading`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info`) and `ThemeButtons` (`primary`, `neutral`, `success`, `danger`), grouped in `MeoCordTheme`. `ThemeOverride` is a part of one, `RootTheme` the app's own theme, and `DeepReadonly<MeoCordTheme>` a theme as code reads it. An app adds tokens by augmenting these interfaces from a module; see [Theming](https://github.com/meocord/meocord/blob/main/README.md#theming). The tokens an app adds have no default, so `RootTheme` requires them, and a role named from `ReservedThemeRole` is refused at the root theme with the role named.
- MeoCord's default theme gives every role a value: colours that keep the hues of 4.0's `Theme` but reach at least 3:1 against every surface an embed or a container sits on in Discord's light, dark, darker and midnight themes (`primary` `#7680F4`, `neutral` `#888B95`, `success` `#26A042`, `warning` `#B08400`, `danger` `#E3606D`, `info` `#1699AE`; 4.0's `warning` `#FFC107` was 1.58:1 on light), the emojis ⏳ ✅ ⚠️ ⛔ ℹ️, and Discord's own button styles for `primary`, `neutral`, `success` and `danger`. A theme's colours, emojis and button styles are checked, and each problem is named with its key path and what to give instead; a role MeoCord reserves is refused in JavaScript as in TypeScript.
- An app sets its theme with `@MeoCord({ theme })`, and `@UseTheme` changes part of it for a controller or a handler: a subclass's over its base class's, a method's over its class's, with `@Controller({ inheritStages: false })` stopping inheritance as it does for guards. A bad token stops the bot before it logs in, naming the decorator. Code reads the call's theme with `useTheme()` from `meocord/common`, in a service or presenter as in the handler, and stages read it as `context.getTheme()`; outside any call it is the running app's theme, or MeoCord's defaults. A bot that sets no `@UseTheme` pays one check per call.
- `bindTheme(fn)` from `meocord/common` runs a callback in the theme of the call that binds it, for a listener a handler registers, such as a collector's `collect` callback, which otherwise runs in its emitter's context.
